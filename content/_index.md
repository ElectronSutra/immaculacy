# Immaculacy

## Preface

This book is on Immaculacy -- a fictional religion from the tabletop RPG
_Exalted_, and an object of my unique preoccupation for the past several years.
In the context of Exalted, Immaculacy is the most widely-practiced and
widely-observed faith in all of Creation. It is the state religion of the Realm,
the most powerful empire in the world. It is the dominant religious and
moral-ethical framework for more characters, both portrayed and unportrayed, in
RY 768 than any other single philosophy or tradition -- except possibly for the
category of 'unorganized local tradition', but that is beyond the scope of what
we must consider. It is an aspect of the setting which _ought_ to play a role in
nearly every player's experience.

Despite this, Immaculacy is sketched out only briefly in the books. We know it
is the religion which derives from the teachings of the Immaculate Dragons, and
we know that it is _useful_ and _successful_ enough that it persists as
Creation's dominant spiritual modus operandi. But in _The Realm_, which
discusses it more than any other book in the Third Edition so far, the topic of
Immaculacy is given only sixteen pages. Four of those pages are devoted to the
structure and stricture of the Immaculate Order, and four to the daily lives of
monks -- leaving, out of the hundreds of pages of 3rd Edition so far, less than
two dozen pages on the topic of the Immaculate experience. We know little about
its beliefs aside from broad strokes, and we know next to nothing about the
rituals and individual experience of being Immaculate.

That is not a criticism of the books -- it is an exciting opportunity for this
one.

This book is written with three personas in mind. First, this book is for a lore
junkie, someone who simply wants to know more about the world of Creation and is
willing to indulge a fan work to fill in those gaps. Second, this book is for a
Storyteller, who wants to use the topic of Immaculacy as part of their game.
Lastly, this book is for players, who want to understand the Immaculate
religious experience more deeply so they can better portray their characters.

The goal of this book is to equip you with a rich resource to add color and
depth to the characters and world of _Exalted_, and to do so in a way which does
not lose sight of the fact that _Exalted_ is first and foremost a game. I have
done what I can to balance my pseudointellectual scholarship against the need
for roleplay-focused and game-focused material.

## Structure of the book

In general, there are two ways to write an overview of a religion. The first way
-- which is the way this book uses -- is a "thematic" overview. In this
approach, you present a series of topics, themes, or _things_ that the religion
has opinions about, and stitch them together into an overview. The other
approach, the "historical" overview, starts at the "beginning" of the tradition
and steps through time to look at how that religion has come to where it is
today. The 3rd edition books give us plenty enough history -- this book is a
thematic study instead.

Each chapter, aside from the introduction, presents a theme or topic that is of
unique concern to Immaculacy. The chapter will present the topic, connect it to
other relevant topics, present various opinions on the topic from different
schools or traditions of Immaculacy, and present some notes on how to use the
topic as part of your roleplay or story planning -- not necessarily in that
order.

The chapters do build on each other, as the topics get more specific and assume
you are familiar with the broader themes. I have tried to structure the chapters
so that you will still get something out of them, even if you haven't read the
earlier ones, but you should probably approach them in order for the most robust
understanding.

As much as possible, I have tried to avoid referencing the real world. However,
that's not possible in the entirety. Many ideas of Immaculacy draw on real-world
religions, especially Buddhist aesthetics, so I cannot avoid referencing the
real world entirely. I have done what I can to minimize the real-world
references, however, to follow a style that is broadly similar to the published
books.

## Notes and acknowledgements

{{% hint info %}}

**About the author**

My name is Sutra. I have been an _Exalted_ fan since I started reading Keychain
of Creation when I was twelve years old. _Exalted_ 1st Edition was my first
tabletop roleplaying game -- I started as the ST and ran a game for my younger
sister (8 at the time), one of my friends, and one of hers. We used little
figurines we made by hand out of child's modeling clay; I still have my sister's
lynx Lunar figurine on a shelf at home.

I am not a scholar of religion; my degrees are in math, computer science, and
software engineering. Everything I write is as an enthusiastic amateur, not as a
qualified expert. But, _Exalted_'s religious landscape has been a unique
hyperfixation of mine for many, many years, so a lot of thought has certainly
gone into this.

I hope it is useful to you.

{{% /hint %}}

{{% hint info %}}

**Bias**

I am an American, but I am not a Christian; as you can probably guess from my
screenname, I am a practicing Buddhist. My religious background _shouldn't_
matter, but of course, we know it does. As an author, my bias will leak into
everything I write.

I supppose the point of this note is to reassure you that "religion," to me, is
a lot more than American Protestantism or what have you. I think that
(especially a lot of the early Exalted material) has a clear lack of deep
religious consideration from the authors; I find this is true of a **lot** of
American media, which assumes "religion" means "American Protestantism" and kind
of goes from there. _Exalted_ can't avoid the topic of Orientalism, so I hope
that my real, personal experience with Buddhism, a major influence on
Immaculacy's aesthetics, helps to deal with some of that.

I am not a religious scholar, but I have participated in a lot of religion.
Buddhist sutra recitations, Catholic mass, praying at mosques, taking langar at
the gurdwara, offering puja at Hindu temples -- I have _experienced_ a lot of
different religions. I am certainly no expert in them, but I can speak from a
broader range of experiences than, I think, many people, and I have tried to
leverage that to make this book as good as it can be.

I hope that shows through in my work, and that you will afford me the credit of
starting from an informed amateur's opinions.
